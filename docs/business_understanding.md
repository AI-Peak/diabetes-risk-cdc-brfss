# Business Understanding

## 1. Project Background

Diabetes is one of the most prevalent chronic diseases worldwide and poses a major public health challenge due to its long-term complications and increasing healthcare costs. Early identification of individuals at high risk enables timely interventions, lifestyle modifications, and preventive healthcare strategies. With the growing availability of large-scale public health survey data, data analytics and machine learning provide valuable tools for identifying important health indicators associated with diabetes risk.

This project utilizes the **CDC Diabetes Health Indicators** dataset, which is derived from the Behavioral Risk Factor Surveillance System (BRFSS), a nationwide health survey conducted by the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC). The dataset contains demographic characteristics, lifestyle behaviors, and general health information collected from adults across the United States, making it suitable for data-driven health analysis and predictive modeling.

---

## 2. Problem Statement

Healthcare professionals and policymakers need reliable evidence to understand which health indicators are most strongly associated with diabetes risk. Although many demographic and lifestyle factors are known to influence diabetes, their relative importance and combined effects remain difficult to interpret using traditional statistical summaries alone.

Furthermore, predictive models often function as "black boxes," providing accurate predictions without sufficient explanations of why a prediction is made. This lack of interpretability limits the practical adoption of machine learning models in healthcare settings where transparency and evidence-based decision making are essential.

Therefore, there is a need for an analytical framework that not only predicts diabetes risk with satisfactory performance but also explains how different health indicators contribute to the model's predictions.

---

## 3. Business Objectives

The primary objectives of this project are:

* Analyze the relationships between demographic characteristics, lifestyle behaviors, and health conditions associated with diabetes.
* Identify the most influential health indicators contributing to diabetes risk.
* Develop and compare multiple machine learning models for diabetes prediction.
* Evaluate model performance using appropriate classification metrics.
* Improve model transparency by applying Explainable Artificial Intelligence (XAI) techniques to interpret prediction results.
* Generate insights that may support preventive healthcare strategies and public health decision making.

---

## 4. Stakeholders

The outcomes of this project may provide value for several stakeholders:

* **Healthcare professionals**, who can better understand factors associated with diabetes risk.
* **Public health organizations**, which may use analytical findings to support prevention programs and awareness campaigns.
* **Researchers and data scientists**, who can use the project as a reference for interpretable healthcare analytics.
* **Students and educators**, who can study an end-to-end machine learning workflow using a real-world public health dataset.

---

## 5. Project Scope

This project focuses on structured data analysis using the CDC Diabetes Health Indicators dataset.

The scope includes:

* Understanding the dataset and its variables.
* Data cleaning and preprocessing.
* Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA).
* Statistical analysis of health indicators.
* Feature engineering where appropriate.
* Development and comparison of machine learning classification models.
* Model evaluation using multiple performance metrics.
* Model interpretation using SHAP for explainable predictions.

The project does not aim to provide medical diagnoses or clinical treatment recommendations. Its findings should be interpreted as data-driven analytical insights rather than clinical evidence.

---

## 6. Expected Outcomes

Upon completion, the project is expected to deliver:

* A well-documented analytical pipeline from raw data to model interpretation.
* Statistical insights into factors associated with diabetes.
* Comparative evaluation of multiple machine learning models.
* Explainable AI visualizations that demonstrate feature contributions to model predictions.
* Actionable analytical findings that can support future research and public health studies.
